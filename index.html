Aim

To analyze and visualize the spatial distribution of gas emissions from dumpsites using an interactive GIS dashboard in Lagos State, Nigeria — strengthening environmental monitoring and informing policy decisions.

Problem Statement

Rapid urbanization in Lagos State has led to significant increases in solid waste generation, resulting in the proliferation of both authorized and unauthorized dumping sites. These sites emit harmful gases including CH₄, CO₂ and other pollutants contributing to environmental degradation, air pollution, and public health risks. A critical gap exists in the inability to systematically detect and monitor unauthorized dumpsites — spatial anomalies in gas concentration may serve as indicators of such activities.

Research Objectives
01Identify and map existing dumpsites within Lagos State
02Estimate and classify gas emission concentrations (CH₄, NO₂, CO) and perform spatial analysis to determine distribution and intensity
03Apply buffer analysis (100 m / 500 m / 1 km) to assess the spatial extent of gas emission impact around dumpsites
04Develop an interactive GIS dashboard for visualization and analysis of dumpsite / landfill gas emissions
05Highlight anomalous high-emission areas that may indicate potential unauthorized dumping sites
Research Questions
What is the spatial distribution of dumpsites in Lagos State?
How are gas emissions distributed across different areas within the state?
Which areas exhibit high concentrations of dumpsite-related gas emissions?
What is the spatial extent of environmental impact around identified dumpsites?
Can spatial anomalies in gas concentration serve as indicators of potential unauthorized dumping sites?
How effective is an interactive GIS dashboard in supporting environmental monitoring and decision-making?

Scope

This study covers Lagos State, Nigeria (2018–present). It focuses on spatial analysis and visualization of gas emissions from landfill sites, incorporating buffer analysis at 100 m, 500 m, and 1 km distances to assess impact zones and building exposure. Anomaly detection uses statistical methods (Z-score) to flag LGAs with emissions significantly above the mean as potential unauthorized dumpsite indicators. Where field measurements are unavailable, modeled and estimated data are used.

Limitations
Reliance on estimated/modeled gas emission data where field measurements are unavailable may affect precision
Spatial interpolation techniques are subject to inaccuracy in areas with sparse data points
Emissions from industrial facilities may influence readings and complicate sole attribution to dumpsites
